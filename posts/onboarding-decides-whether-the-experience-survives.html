Onboarding decides whether the experience survives

20 April 2026 · 2 min read · modern-l-d, work, learning-design

Teams rarely need more more visible activity. They need a workflow that makes better decisions in live work easier to notice and reinforce.

Teams build compelling experiences, but users still arrive confused because onboarding and guidance break before the core experience begins.

So the real design question is whether the system strengthens better decisions in live work where the work actually happens.

What evidence from adjacent fields supports or weakens this claim: teams rarely need more more visible activity.

The adjacent evidence from learning science, behavior science, organizational design, service design points in the same direction: outcomes improve when the workflow changes, not just the message around it.

In this post, Baldwin & Ford (1988) is the baseline reference and canonical frame for transfer and maintenance of learned capability.

What matters is the point where intent becomes action: the point in the workflow where people choose what to do next.

The tradeoff is more visible activity versus better decisions in live work: teams can create visible momentum while leaving the next decision that actually shapes performance untouched.

Treat the workflow decision as the proof point. Keep the design only if it improves your next round of execution and the baseline metric attached to it.
