Why delight belongs inside the core loop

20 April 2026 · 2 min read · modern-l-d, work, learning-design

The real tab-management problem is not clutter alone. It is whether the product reduces guilt and cognitive load without violating the user's sense of control.

Teams ship functional experiences, but users still feel friction, guilt, or indifference because emotional design is treated as an extra.

Work in learning science, behavior science, organizational design, management science keeps returning to the same test: does the design improve what people notice, choose, and correct in real conditions?

Baldwin & Ford (1988) provides a foundational signal here and canonical frame for transfer and maintenance of learned capability.

What matters is the emotional friction inside the interaction: users need relief and control, not a system that tidies things on their behalf.

The tradeoff is between cleaner system behavior and user trust: an interface can become technically neater while making people feel less safe using it.
