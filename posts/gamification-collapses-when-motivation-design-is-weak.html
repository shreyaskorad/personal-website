Across learning science, behavior science, organizational design, management science, the same pattern appears: the intervention matters only if it changes behavior around a live decision.

In this post, Grossman & Salas (2011) is the baseline reference and keeps the bar on transfer into work behavior.

What matters is the point where intent becomes action: the moment someone decides whether to engage, return, or try again.

The real risk is confusing visible mechanics for evidence that motivation, recovery, and repeat behavior has improved.

Start with one recurring workflow moment where the next attempt people choose to make keeps slipping. Redesign that moment before changing anything broader If you test it, track 1 workflow metric against a baseline that shows whether your next decision improved.

Use one live decision as the test. If your workflow metric improves against the baseline, keep the design. If it only increases activity, drop it.
