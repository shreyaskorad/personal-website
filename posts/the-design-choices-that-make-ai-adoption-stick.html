The design choices that make AI adoption stick

20 April 2026 · 2 min read · ai, modern-l-d, work

The issue is not faster content output. It is whether the design improves the human handoff after automation under real constraints.

Teams keep adopting the capability decisions that make ai useful tools, but decision quality in daily workflows is not improving.

Across learning science, behavior science, organizational design, human-computer interaction, the same pattern appears: the intervention matters only if it changes behavior around a live decision.

In this post, Brynjolfsson, Li, & Raymond (2023) is the baseline reference and concrete evidence on how generative AI changes work quality and productivity.

The design succeeds or fails at the moment work moves from automation back to a person, which is where the human handoff after automation is actually shaped.

The real risk is confusing faster content output for evidence that better human judgment inside the workflow has improved.

Use one live decision as the test. If your workflow metric improves against the baseline, keep the design. If it only increases activity, drop it.
