The mechanism teams miss when they chase activity

19 April 2026 · 2 min read · modern-l-d, work, learning-design

Activity is not proof of learning. The real signal is whether people make better decisions in the same workflow one week later.

Teams usually do not have an effort problem. They have a transfer problem. People complete training, attend sessions, and generate visible activity, but the same decision errors keep repeating in live work.

The missing mechanism is the decision moment itself. If the intervention does not change what someone notices, chooses, and corrects under real constraints, activity metrics can rise while capability stays flat.

Transfer research has made this point for years: learning sticks when people have manager support, opportunity to apply, and follow-up inside the workflow. Without those conditions, completion data creates false confidence.

Behavior design reaches the same conclusion from another angle. Better outcomes come from redesigning cues, feedback, and friction around target behavior, not from adding more content around it.

A practical test is straightforward. Pick one recurring decision that often goes wrong. Redesign that moment for two weeks with clearer prompts, faster feedback, and one explicit review ritual.

Then measure judgement quality, not just participation. Did rework drop? Did escalation quality improve? Did handoffs get cleaner? Those are capability signals.

If those signals improve, the intervention is working. If only activity goes up, the mechanism is still missing.
